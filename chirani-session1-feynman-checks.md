## Day 1: Defect vs Failure

**Session jargon I am not allowed to use:** Defect, failure

**Plain-language explanation:**

A problem can exist inside an application even before someone notices it. For example, a calculation might be written incorrectly, but the problem becomes visible only when someone enters a particular amount and receives the wrong total.

**Real-life analogy:**

A calculator has a faulty internal component, but you notice the problem only when it displays the wrong answer for a calculation.

**What goes wrong when it is ignored or misused:**

In LearnLanka, a student might book a tutoring session but receive an incorrect confirmation because of a problem in the application.

**Where my explanation broke (the gap):**

I need to practise distinguishing a problem inside the software from the incorrect behaviour that a user observes.

## Day 2: Traceability

**Session jargon I am not allowed to use:** Traceability

**Plain-language explanation:**

It should be possible to connect a requirement to the checks created for it and to the results of those checks. This helps us see whether every important request has been checked.

**Real-life analogy:**

A shopping receipt lets you connect the items you purchased to the amount you paid.

**What goes wrong when it is ignored or misused:**

In BookSwap, a login requirement might have no corresponding check, allowing a login problem to go unnoticed.

**Where my explanation broke (the gap):**

I need more practice linking individual requirements to their related checks and results.

## Day 3: Response Time

**Session jargon I am not allowed to use:** Response time

**Plain-language explanation:**

This is how long someone waits between asking an application to do something and seeing it respond. An application can show the correct information but still be frustrating if the user waits too long.

**Real-life analogy:**

Ordering food at a restaurant may be successful, but waiting an unreasonable amount of time makes the experience frustrating.

**What goes wrong when it is ignored or misused:**

In BookSwap, students might abandon a search if available books take too long to appear.

**Where my explanation broke (the gap):**

I need to understand how to determine an acceptable waiting time from the requirements.

## Day 4: Severity vs Priority

**Session jargon I am not allowed to use:** Severity, priority

**Plain-language explanation:**

One problem may cause serious damage, while another may need fixing sooner because it affects an urgent task. These are two different ways of deciding how to handle problems.

**Real-life analogy:**

A broken emergency exit is dangerous, while a missing sign for a meeting room may be less serious. The emergency exit problem needs urgent attention.

**What goes wrong when it is ignored or misused:**

In GreenChit, a problem preventing employees from submitting expense claims could be overlooked if the team does not assess its impact and urgency correctly.

**Where my explanation broke (the gap):**

I need to practise deciding which problems are most serious and which need attention first.

## Day 5: Release Sign-off

**Session jargon I am not allowed to use:** Release sign-off

**Plain-language explanation:**

Before making an application available to users, the responsible people review the testing results, known problems, and remaining risks to decide whether it is ready to be released.

**Real-life analogy:**

Before a bus begins a journey, the responsible person checks that it is ready and considers whether it is safe to travel.

**What goes wrong when it is ignored or misused:**

In GreenChit, releasing the application without reviewing a serious expense-claim problem could affect employees who depend on it.

**Where my explanation broke (the gap):**

I need to learn which test results and unresolved problems must be considered before deciding that a release is ready.
